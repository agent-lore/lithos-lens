# Lithos Lens User Manual

Lithos Lens is a web window onto **Lithos**: the tasks agents are working on, the dependency graph between them, the findings they post, and the knowledge notes and the typed edges between those notes. It updates live as Lithos emits events. Reading never changes anything. A small set of curated write actions (create a task, cancel, reopen, add a dependency, proceed past a gate) is available once you choose an operator identity, and every one of them shows you its consequences before anything is written.

## Getting around

Every page shares the same slim header. On the left, **Lithos Lens** is the brand link — it always returns you to the Tasks dashboard — and beside it a small badge names the environment this instance serves (here, `DEV`). In the middle, the **operator chip** shows who you are acting as: `choose an operator to act` until you pick an identity, then `Acting as <name>` with a `switch` link (see [Acting as an operator](#acting-as-an-operator)). On the right is the primary navigation.

![](screenshots/dashboard.png)

**Tasks**, **Graph** and **Knowledge** are live, and the one you are on is highlighted. **Settings** is a placeholder for a future milestone and is greyed out. The **Search knowledge** box on the far right takes you straight to knowledge search from any page. Task pages also carry a **Live updates connected** indicator under the title: while it is lit, Lens is subscribed to Lithos's event stream and the board refreshes itself as tasks change; if the connection drops it says so and reconnects on its own.

## The Tasks dashboard

The dashboard is the home page (both `/` and `/tasks` show it): the operational view of every task Lithos knows about, sorted into what needs you, what is moving, what is ready, what is waiting, and what has recently finished. The screenshots in this section are narrowed to one project and to open tasks only; an unfiltered board on a busy Lithos is long.

### Reading the board

![](screenshots/dashboard.png)

Across the top, a row of **summary cards** gives the shape of the workload: **Needs attention** (tasks promoted out of their section because a rule fired), **In progress** (active claims), **Ready** (unclaimed and workable now), **Blocked** (waiting on predecessors or gates), **Gates** (open gates holding work), **Recently completed** and **Recently cancelled** (resolved inside the time window shown on the card), and **Agents** (the size of the Lithos registry). The counts respect whatever filters you have applied.

Two strips sit under the filter bar. **Epics** lists every open epic that has tasks on the current board, each with a progress bar and a `done/total` count; click one to scope the board to that epic. **Projects** lists every project with a task count; the selected project is outlined, and **Clear** drops the project filter.

The tasks themselves are grouped into sections, each with a count in its heading:

- **Needs attention** — tasks Lens has promoted out of their natural section. The reason is shown as a chip under the row (for example `stale-open`, with "Open 63d with no resolution"). Unsatisfiable blockers and dependency cycles are always flagged; the time-based rules (a human gate waiting too long, a claim about to lapse, a stale open task, a ready task nobody has picked up) use thresholds set in the Lens configuration.
- **Gates** — open gates, grouped by kind (for example `PR 1`). A gate row shows what it is waiting for, how long it has been waiting, who watches it, its metadata, and how many tasks it blocks.
- **In progress** — tasks with an active claim, showing the claiming agent.
- **Ready** — unclaimed tasks whose blockers are all satisfied.
- **Blocked** — tasks waiting on something; each lists what it is **blocked by**, with a link to the blocker.
- **Completed** and **Cancelled** — terminal tasks resolved inside the window, newest first, with their resolution time.

Every row shows the task title, the leading part of its description (rendered as Markdown and cut at a configurable budget, with **… see more** leading to the full text), the short id, a status badge, the creating agent and creation time, a claim badge (`unclaimed` or the claiming agent) and the task's **tag chips**. Clicking a tag chip adds that tag to the filter; clicking the title opens the [detail page](#task-detail). The **⋯** at the foot of a row is the row menu, which carries the write actions for that task when you are acting as an operator.

Because Lens is subscribed to Lithos events, rows appear, move between sections and update their badges on their own. A banner tells you when the data shown may be partial — for example when the number of open rows exceeds the configured cap, or when Lithos rejected a filter value.

### Filtering what you see

![](screenshots/dashboard-filters.png)

The filter bar narrows the board:

- **Status** — tick **open**, **completed** and/or **cancelled** to choose which sections are shown.
- **Project** — one or more project slugs; the Projects strip sets the same thing with a click.
- **Tag** — a tag such as `area:docs`. Clicking a tag chip on a row does the same thing.
- **Agent (creator or claimer)** — restrict to tasks one agent created or holds. Agents that have been inactive for a while are hidden from the picker; **Show all agents** reveals them.
- **Created since** — a start date applied to every task by creation time.
- **Resolved since** — the window for the Completed and Cancelled sections and the two "Recently …" cards.

Click **Apply filters** to update the board, or **Reset** to clear everything. The current filter lives in the page's URL query string, so a filtered view is shareable, bookmarkable, and survives a refresh or the browser's back and forward buttons.

## The dependency graph

**Graph** draws one scope's dependency structure from Lithos's task edges. Open it from the navigation and you are asked to **pick a scope**: a graph renders one **project** or one **open epic** at a time, and both lists are on the page.

![](screenshots/task-graph.png)

The **legend** explains the marks. `A → B` means A blocks B (B cannot start until A is done). `A ⇢ B` means B waits on gate A. `A ▸ B` means A is B's parent (hierarchy, never satisfied). `A ⋯ B` means B was discovered while working on A. A dashed **ghost** is a task outside the scope, drawn once with its project name so the picture stays honest about edges that leave the scope; its own edges are never fetched.

The toolbar under the title holds the controls:

- **Show/Hide resolved tasks** — completed and cancelled tasks are hidden by default.
- **Show/Hide isolated tasks** — tasks with no edges at all.
- **Show hierarchy** and **Show provenance** — overlay parent/child and discovered-from edges on top of the blocking edges.
- **Find a task** — type a title or id to locate a node.
- **Show as text** — the same graph as a layered list: each layer, the blockers of every task, any **cycle** bracketed in its layer with everything below it marked "blocked via cycle", and the ghosts. The text view is the complete picture; the canvas only ever shows part of a large graph (drag to pan).

Below the canvas, **Longest blocking chain** names the longest run of blocked-by edges inside the scope, with each task's short id, which is the quickest way to see what is really holding a project up. The **as of** time tells you when the edges were read.

## Task detail

Click any row title to open a task's detail page. It gathers everything Lithos holds about one task, and it is also where the write actions for that task live.

![](screenshots/task-detail.png)

From the top: the task's epic, if it has one, as a link; the title; the short id, status and type (`task`, `epic` or `gate`); who created it and when; the resolution time for a terminal task; and the tag chips. The **Description** is rendered Markdown, never truncated.

**Nearby graph** is a small canvas of the task's neighbourhood — blockers two hops up and dependents one hop down — with a link to **open this task in the project graph**. **Dependencies** lists what this task waits on (each with its status, and for a gate its kind and whether it is satisfied); **Blocks** lists what waits on it. **Provenance** shows the task this one was **discovered while working on**, and the follow-ons it **spawned**. **Metadata** is the task's metadata map, including whatever the agents that worked on it recorded (branches, run ids, costs, PR links). **Active Claims** shows who holds the task and until when. **Findings** is the [finding timeline](#finding-timeline).

Use **← Back to tasks** at the top to return to the board with your filters intact.

## Finding timeline

Findings are the messages agents post against a task as they work on it: progress, results, review verdicts, gate resolutions. The timeline is the audit trail of what happened to a task and why.

![](screenshots/findings.png)

The timeline sits at the foot of the detail page, oldest first. Each entry shows the posting agent, the timestamp and the finding's text, rendered as Markdown so links to pull requests, branches and other tasks are clickable. Automated agents prefix their findings with a bracketed tag (for example `[DevelopResult]`, `[ExternalReview]`, `[GateResolved]`), so you can scan a long timeline by kind. New findings appear as they are posted while the page is open.

## Acting as an operator

Lens shows you Lithos without any identity. To act — create a task, cancel or reopen one, add a dependency, proceed past a gate — you first choose an **operator identity** from the chip in the header (or by opening `/operator`).

![](screenshots/operator.png)

An operator id is a lowercase slug (letters, digits and dashes). It is stored in a browser cookie and is an **attribution label**: it names who a write is recorded as in Lithos, and nothing more. The page says it plainly: Lens has no authentication or authorization, anyone who can reach the port can perform these actions, and it is built for a single operator on a trusted local network. An id that already belongs to an agent is refused rather than silently reused, and writes are never attributed to Lens's own service agent.

Once an identity is set, write affordances appear in context:

- **New task** on the dashboard opens a form for a task, epic or gate: title, type, Markdown description, project, tags, an optional parent, and a list of tasks it is blocked by. The form carries its own request id, so submitting it twice creates one task.

![](screenshots/task-new.png)

- **Cancel…** on an open task's detail page (and in the row menu **⋯**) first shows what cancelling would strand and whose claims it would release, then asks you to confirm.
- **Reopen** on a completed task puts it back to open; the page warns that tasks which became ready when it completed will be blocked again.
- **Add a dependency** on a task's detail page takes a relation (this task is blocked by, or blocks, another) and the other task's id, or at least six characters of it. **Check relation…** shows both titles and what the edge means for readiness before anything is written.
- **Proceed anyway…** on a gate row completes the gate by hand, overriding whatever watches it.

Every action goes through a confirmation page and ends with a receipt. If Lithos refuses a write, the refusal and its reason are shown rather than retried. When Lens's own guards cannot vouch for an action it offers **Proceed anyway…** with the reason spelled out.

## Knowledge

**Knowledge** is the entry point to the notes Lithos holds. The landing page has a **search box**, a row of **namespace facets** with counts (click one to narrow a search to that namespace), links to **Browse tags**, **Browse the graph** and the count of **unresolved contradictions**, and, before you search, a list of your recent notes.

![](screenshots/knowledge.png)

A search result shows the note's title, type, status (`active` or `archived`), namespace and confidence, its path in the knowledge store, a snippet, when it was updated, and an **in graph** link that opens the note as the centre of the knowledge graph. Click the title to read the note.

![](screenshots/knowledge-tags.png)

**Tags** lists every tag with the number of notes carrying it, most-used first. The **Filter** box narrows the list by prefix (the chips above it offer the common prefixes such as `project:` or `source:`), and long lists tell you how many more there are so you can narrow further. Click a tag to search for notes that carry it.

## Knowledge notes

A note page renders one Lithos knowledge note. You reach it from a search result, from a finding or task that links to it, or from the knowledge graph.

![](screenshots/note.png)

The header shows the title, the note's type and status, its namespace and confidence, who wrote it and when it was created and last updated, and its tags. **Related** names the notes this one links to or is linked from, and **open in graph** draws the note with its neighbours in the [knowledge graph](#the-knowledge-graph). The body is the note's Markdown, with wiki-links turned into links to the notes they name wherever Lens can resolve them.

The **← Back** link returns you to wherever you came from — the search you ran, the task whose finding linked here, or the graph.

## The knowledge graph

The knowledge graph draws the typed edges between notes: `supports`, `refines`, `derived_from`, `contradicts`, `depends_on` and the rest. Open it from **Browse the graph** on the knowledge page, from **in graph** on a search result, or from **open in graph** on a note.

Before you choose a scope, the page shows the whole edge table as two summaries — edges by **type** and edges by **namespace** — and a scope picker: type a scope, or pick an edge type and a namespace, then **Draw**. The edge table is cached, and the page tells you how old it is.

![](screenshots/knowledge-graph.png)

A drawn graph has a toolbar: **Full page** (below), a **min weight** slider that hides weak edges and reports how many it hid, **provenance** checkboxes to include or exclude `inferred` and `declared` edges, **colour notes by** namespace or note type, and a **Find** box for a title in the graph. The canvas colours notes by the chosen key, sizes them by their connections in this view, greys out archived notes, and draws each edge type in its own style; the key under the canvas explains the colours and line styles.

Click a note to open its **node panel**: the note's type, status, namespace and confidence, its degree in this view, **Centre on this** to redraw the graph around it, and its relations grouped by edge type with the weight of each. Where a note has neighbours outside the view, the panel offers **Show its neighbours**, and **Collapse** to take them away again. Click an edge to open its **edge panel**: a sentence stating the relation, its type, weight, namespace, provenance (declared in frontmatter, or inferred and by what) and creation time. Panels are plain links, so they work from the keyboard and the URL you share reproduces the selection.

Under the canvas, **Legend** defines each edge type in a sentence, **Edges by type** lists every drawn edge as `A → B (weight)` with both titles linked, and **Not shown** accounts for every edge the weight or provenance filters hid. The **Show as text** view carries the same information without the canvas.

![](screenshots/knowledge-graph-full.png)

**Full page** hands the whole window to the canvas. The toolbar folds into a drawer on the left with the same controls, a **Text view** link and the **Key**; the navigation stays reachable. If the edge table changes while you are looking, a **graph changed — refresh** pill appears rather than the drawing moving under you.

## When Lithos is unavailable

Lens stays up when Lithos does not. If the Lithos connection is down or degraded, every task page replaces its content with a clear notice — **Lithos is offline or degraded. Task data is unavailable. Lens is running and will retry on refresh.** — and a status strip showing the four components: **Lens**, **Lithos**, **Events** (the live update stream, which keeps reconnecting on its own) and **LLM** (the optional summarisation helper, `disabled` unless configured).

![](screenshots/degraded.png)

Knowledge pages behave the same way. Nothing is cached stale and shown as if it were current. Check that the Lithos server is running and that Lens's configured Lithos URL points at it; the same four values are available as JSON from `/health` for a monitor to poll. Once Lithos is back, a refresh restores the page and the live indicator relights.
